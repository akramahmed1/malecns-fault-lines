Fault Lines in a Fly Brain

Mapping structural resilience in sensory-to-motor pathways across the complete male Drosophila central nervous system

A focused, defensible study built around the MaleCNS v1.0 connectome. The paper asks a simple question: which parts of the fly nervous system can lose connections and keep a route from sensation to action, and which parts fail when only a few critical neurons are removed?

01 | Research decision

The candidate question is resilience, not whether a connectome can play a game

The dataset is new, but connectome-shaped controllers already exist. A formal literature review must determine whether the proposed whole-CNS resilience study is novel enough to pursue.

Working title: Fault Lines in a Fly Brain: Mapping Structural Resilience in the Complete Male Drosophila Connectome

What the paper does

  • Builds a directed, weighted graph from MaleCNS v1.0.
  • Traces well-annotated sensory populations to descending and motor populations.
  • Measures redundant routes, minimum cuts, and pathway loss under simulated lesions.
  • Compares biological wiring with controlled rewired graphs.
  • Publishes code, processed graph tables, and an interactive pathway viewer.

What the paper does not claim

  • That synapse counts reveal real-time neural activity.
  • That a static wiring diagram reproduces learning or behavior.
  • That one male specimen represents the full species.
  • That a simulated lesion predicts a clinical or biological outcome.
  • That structural sex differences alone explain behavior.

The gap this fills

The final Cell article already includes sensorimotor max-flow analysis [1], so basic flow analysis is not a novel contribution. Two 2026 arXiv preprints add relevant prior art: FlyGM reports a connectome-shaped controller for simulated fly locomotion [5], while FLYNN reports robustness experiments in fly-topology robot navigation [6]. The distinct lesion-response and structural-resilience benchmark remains a candidate contribution until a formal literature review establishes novelty.

Novelty gate: Run a dated search across peer-reviewed databases and preprint servers before coding. If an existing study already reports the same MaleCNS whole-CNS lesion benchmark, narrow the study to a modality, such as vision-to-flight control, or to verified dimorphic versus shared circuitry.

02 | Research questions

Four tests turn the idea into a falsifiable study

IDQuestionTestable hypothesis
RQ1How redundant are sensory-to-motor routes across annotated modalities?H1: Route redundancy will differ by modality after controlling for graph size and degree.
RQ2Which neurons, cell types, and regions produce the largest structural failures when removed?H2: Removing high-dominator or high-flow-betweenness nodes will reduce reachability more than random or degree-matched removal.
RQ3Does biological wiring preserve sensorimotor communication better than plausible null graphs?H3: The observed graph will retain more weighted reachability and disjoint routes than degree-preserving rewires at matched lesion levels.
RQ4Are dimorphic and male-specific pathways organized around more localized bottlenecks than shared pathways?H4: Candidate dimorphic subnetworks will show higher concentration of path dependence. This remains exploratory and must be limited to cross-sex mappings verified in the paper or official dataset.

Primary outcome

The main endpoint is the area under the robustness curve for weighted source-to-target reachability as nodes or edges are removed. Each curve will be reported with confidence intervals from repeated perturbation runs.

Secondary outcomes

  • Number of vertex-disjoint and edge-disjoint routes
  • Minimum vertex and edge cut size
  • Weighted shortest-path efficiency
  • Communicability between source and target sets
  • Fraction of target neurons still reachable
  • Critical-node rank stability across metrics
  • Effect size against matched null graphs
  • Sensitivity to synapse thresholds and edge weights

Decision rule: If H3 is not supported, that is still a useful result. The paper can show that apparent biological robustness is explained by degree distribution rather than a special wiring pattern.

Initial pathway panels

Start only with pathways whose source and target annotations are clear. The visual panel may use Janelia's official R1-R6 to DNg13 visual-to-motor example as a documented case [4]. It is an anatomical pathway example, not evidence that the static connectome reproduces dynamic behavior. Add olfactory, gustatory, mechanosensory, and proprioceptive panels only after an annotation audit confirms usable coverage.

Scope boundary: The graph represents structural connectivity. Synapse count is a weight proxy, not a measured firing rate. Any dynamic model belongs in a later paper after physiological assumptions can be justified.

04 | Analytical method

A four-stage pipeline keeps every result traceable

1. IngestDownload approved MaleCNS tables, preserve release metadata, and checksum every raw file.
2. AuditCheck duplicates, orphan IDs, missing labels, edge direction, and synapse-weight distributions.
3. AnalyzeBuild pathway subgraphs, calculate resilience metrics, and run lesion experiments.
4. ExplainRank bottlenecks, visualize failure routes, and report uncertainty and sensitivity.

Graph construction

Represent every neuron as a node and each documented directed synaptic connection as an edge. Use summed synapse counts as the base edge weight. Do not add electrical-coupling data unless a separate verified source is available, and never force it into the same directed model. Record each filter in a machine-readable manifest.

Threshold plan

Run the primary analysis at a prespecified minimum synapse count chosen after the distribution audit. Repeat it across at least three thresholds, including the unthresholded graph, to show whether conclusions depend on weak edges.

Pathway definitions

  • Sources: modality-specific sensory populations with clear annotations.
  • Targets: descending neurons for brain-to-cord analysis and motor neurons for end-to-end analysis.
  • Allowed paths: directed paths within a biologically reasonable hop limit, reported as a sensitivity parameter rather than a hidden rule.
  • Compartments: full CNS, brain-to-descending, and descending-to-motor segments.

Null models

Degree-preserving rewires test whether resilience follows simply from each neuron's number of inputs and outputs.

Region-constrained rewires preserve coarse anatomy while breaking exact neuron-to-neuron structure.

Reproducibility requirement: Fix random seeds, save every graph transform, publish environment files, and make all reported figures regenerable from one command.

Statistical plan

  • Report median differences, standardized effect sizes, and 95 percent bootstrap confidence intervals.
  • Use permutation tests for observed-versus-null comparisons.
  • Control the false discovery rate when testing many cell types or regions.
  • Separate confirmatory tests for H1-H3 from exploratory analysis for H4.
  • Publish negative and null findings rather than selecting only visually dramatic circuits.
Compute plan: Begin with sparse graph operations and pathway panels. Full all-pairs calculations are unnecessary. Scale only after profiling memory, runtime, and the number of reachable source-target pairs.

Minimum viable paper

One visual pathway, one additional well-annotated modality, three lesion strategies, one degree-preserving null model, a complete sensitivity analysis, and reproducible code. The paper should expand only after this core result is stable.

08 | Risks and decisions

The paper is strongest when its limitations shape the design

RiskWhat it meansDesign response
Single specimenIndividual variation cannot be estimated from MaleCNS alone.Frame claims as properties of this mapped connectome and compare only where matched data are valid.
Static structureEdges do not supply membrane dynamics, timing, neuromodulation, or behavior.Use structural terms such as reachability and resilience. Do not call the result functional proof.
Weight ambiguitySynapse count is not the same as physiological influence.Compare binary, count-weighted, and optional transmitter-aware specifications.
Annotation gapsSome source or target labels may be incomplete or uncertain.Publish inclusion rules and repeat analysis after removing uncertain groups.
ScaleNaive all-pairs algorithms may be expensive on 166,700 nodes.Use sparse matrices, bounded pathway panels, and profiling before full runs.
Novelty overlapRecent work is appearing quickly.Freeze the final question only after a dated literature matrix and advisor review.

Go or no-go checkpoints

  • GO IF: The official release provides stable IDs, synapse edges, and enough functional labels to define at least two pathways.
  • GO IF: The literature screen finds no study with the same MaleCNS whole-CNS robustness benchmark.
  • NARROW IF: Labels support only one modality. Publish a deep visual sensorimotor study instead of a broad atlas.
  • STOP IF: Results change direction under reasonable thresholds or null models and cannot be explained transparently.

Best next action: Complete the novelty matrix and data dictionary first. Those two documents determine whether this becomes a credible paper or just an attractive demo.
